# Tone of Voice

Every piece of Pearson/RevOps copy — headlines, captions, chart titles, emails, Slack updates, report narration — should sound like this, not just look on-brand.

## Core attributes

| Attribute | What it means |
|---|---|
| Empathetic | Acknowledge the reader's/learner's journey; lead with understanding |
| Clear and direct | Say what you mean, plainly and concisely |
| Motivating | Inspire action and confidence; focus on what's possible |
| Human | Warm, real, conversational — never corporate or robotic |

## Rules

- Write in American English for all global/corporate output.
- Active voice, always — "we found that…" not "it was found that…"
- Concrete, outcome-focused statements over abstract claims.
- Plain language — write for the audience, not to sound impressive.
- One concept or theme per communication — don't cram three messages into one headline.
- Generous white space in text-heavy documents to aid scanning.

## Words to avoid → use instead

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Leverage | Use |
| Synergy | Collaboration / working together |
| Solutions | The specific thing itself |
| Empower (overused) | Sparingly, and only when concrete |
| "Industry-leading," "best-in-class" | Specific, evidence-backed statements |
| Passive voice ("it was found that…") | Active voice ("we found that…") |
| Unexplained jargon/acronyms | Plain language; spell out on first use |

## Anchor line

All messaging should trace back to Pearson's purpose: *"Helping people realize the life they imagine through learning."* This applies even to a report caption or a dashboard tooltip — the connection doesn't need to be stated outright, but the tone should never contradict it (e.g., never cynical, never purely transactional).
